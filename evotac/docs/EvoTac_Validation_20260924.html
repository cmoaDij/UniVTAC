π0.5 baseline definition

FTP-1 is the tactile expert used in historical comparisons; its released policy consumes tactile streams. It is not a fair tactile-free base policy. This transition uses the released lerobot/pi05_base checkpoint at revision b211f3d44c36b6acfcf7ae94a64e8e96f75a64ba and exposes only head RGB, wrist RGB, language, and 8-D proprioception. The student targets are accepted joint targets distilled from train rollouts whose teachers may be FTP-1, SAC, or scripted recovery; that teacher provenance is recorded and is not described as a vision-only demonstration source.

Completed software and data work

  • policy/pi05_adapter.py has an explicit RGB/state contract, aspect-preserving 224-pixel preprocessing, relative/absolute action decoding, and no tactile input.
  • scripts/pi05_worker.py refuses missing or tiny weights and loads safetensors strictly; the LeRobot fallback that could return random weights is not used.
  • scripts/export_pi05_dataset.py audits train split parents, 120/20-Hz alignment, joint order/units, accepted target provenance, camera freshness, and source hashes. It never reads the tactile subtree. Statistics are computed from train only.
  • Export completed as datasets/pi05_insert_hole_train_20260924_c: 44 unique train parents, 7,082 aligned frames, 136 excluded duplicate/ineligible records, and a train-only stats.json. This generated dataset remains untracked.
  • Added no-tactile invariance and action-contract tests. The relevant test subset currently passes.
  • Snapshot gate changes require a completed strict dev/acceptance audit, matching frozen protocol and checkpoint hashes, and disjoint test parents. Test launch remains fail-closed.

Facts and project inferences

  • π0.5 is documented as a vision-language-action flow model with base checkpoints intended for fine-tuning; the official OpenPI repository reports >8 GB inference memory and >70 GB for full fine-tuning, with LoRA above 22.5 GB. OpenPI
  • FTP-1 is a generalist tactile foundation policy whose stated inputs include heterogeneous tactile signals. FTP-1 paper
  • Demonstration-guided learning is a standard response to sparse-reward exploration difficulty. Nair et al.
  • For this project, the sparse positive recovery count, actor drift, and failed handoff are hypotheses requiring controlled experiments; none is proof of causation.
